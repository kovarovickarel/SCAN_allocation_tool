# SCAN Allocation Tool: Agent Guide

Read this file before changing the application. It describes the current product, code layout, domain model, and constraints that matter when adding features.

## Product overview

The SCAN Allocation Tool is a client-facing React app for planning tooling workpackages, estimating project effort, and allocating engineering and management capacity across projects and months. Users can organize workpackages by tool and project, configure effort assumptions, manage team members, inspect monthly timelines, and adjust allocations. The app also has basic, extended, and retro visual modes.

This is a front-end-only application with no application backend. Currency conversion calls the public Frankfurter exchange-rate API; all other planning is local. Workpackages, projects, team members, and configuration values live in React state for the current session. The two compact-layout preferences are stored in `localStorage` by `src/hooks/useAppViewState.ts`; the rest of the planning data is not persisted across reloads.

The project began as a client-provided monolithic TSX application. Its original source is retained at `legacy/Initial_Monolith_SCAN_allocation_tool.tsx` as a reference. The active application is under `src/`.

## Main user workflows

- Create and edit workpackages, including tool-specific options and custom `Other` effort, duration, and maintenance assumptions.
- Drag workpackages from the unassigned pool into projects and reorder projects.
- Manage team members, roles, tool associations, and individual FTE capacity.
- Review project effort estimates and project staffing timelines.
- Click the project cost amount to review monthly and cumulative allocated spending, with tool, workpackage, management support, and member breakdowns.
- Review combined team timelines and assign or adjust member allocations.
- Team timeline allocations are staged locally. `Save & Close` applies the draft; `Close`, `Discard & Close`, the header close button, Escape, and backdrop clicks discard it. Saving an allocation inside a nested dialog updates the timeline draft only.
- Override monthly values in timelines, reset overrides, and save or cancel edits.
- Configure FTE rates, management overhead, stability, reusability, and other defaults.
- Switch tool views, layout density, app mode, and visual theme.

## Domain notes

- A project has a start month, duration, stability factor, milestones, hidden tools/subcategories, and custom monthly management overrides.
- Projects support optional `isRFQ` (false by default), selected during creation or inline project editing. `ProjectRFQBadge` appears immediately after the product type in project cards, project staffing timelines, and team timeline project rows. RFQ means Request for Quotation, not yet won or nominated by the client; it is informational only and does not change effort, costs, capacity, or automatic allocation priorities.
- A workpackage belongs to a tool, can be assigned to a project, and may have custom member assignments, monthly assignments, and monthly effort overrides.
- A team member has an FTE capacity, tool, footprint, and role (`engineering`, `management`, or `both`).
- Expanded team member cards measure the space left beside location/supplier tags. `ResponsiveMemberName` shows the full name when it fits, otherwise first initial plus surname, falling back to initials in very narrow cards. Hovering the name or card reveals the full name. The visible name occupies only its displayed text width so location and supplier tags follow it without empty space. Measure available capacity from the full row minus sibling tags and gaps, independently of the selected label, to avoid abbreviation oscillation. Hidden label measurements, ResizeObserver cleanup, and font-load remeasurement keep names stable and restore full names after resizing.
- External members show a supplier tag directly after their location tag in expanded team member cards and timeline member labels. `MemberSupplierBadge` resolves names from configured suppliers and matches the non-FTE supplier tag styling. Compact member cards retain their initials-only layout.
- External members also require a `supplierId` from the current configured supplier list when created or edited. The member form shows a required supplier selector only for external members; switching to internal clears the saved supplier association, while toggling within the unsaved draft retains the selection. Suppliers referenced by external members are protected from removal, configuration saving, and Reset to Defaults alongside non-FTE purchase suppliers. Legacy external records without a supplier must select one before saving edits; do not silently assign a supplier.
- Team members also have optional `isExternal`, defaulting to false. The add/edit member dialog saves it, and the red `ExternalMemberBadge` appears next to the role in the member pool and team timeline. The compact checkbox sits below the name fields without a background or border; its label matches the name field labels in size, color, and weight. External members require a defined, finite nonnegative `monthlySalaryCost` before saving. The conditional salary field accepts decimal commas and saves two-decimal amounts with `monthlySalaryCurrency`, initialized from the current FTE cost currency for new records and retained when editing. Internal members do not require a salary, and toggling External within the draft retains the entered value. Any positive active allocation triggers the full monthly salary as non-FTE spending. Salary uses its saved currency and ignores location rates. Charge once per member/calendar month; split across projects, workpackages, management and tool views in proportion to their allocations. Months with no positive active allocations incur no salary. Allocation eligibility and capacity rules remain unchanged.
- Configuration includes `fteCosts: FteCostSettings`, with one selected currency (EUR by default) and hourly rates keyed by `FOOTPRINTS` location codes. Default EUR/hour rates are PRA 60, BIE 80, CHE 20, TRO 115, CAI 40, and TOK 55. Rates accept finite nonnegative decimals including 0; blank rates are null (unconfigured), and saved values remain in session state. The FTE costs tab normalizes commas to dots and disables saving invalid rates; Cancel discards the draft and Reset to Defaults restores the EUR rates. Rates do not change effort or existing allocations automatically; both Magic Wands use them as a secondary cost objective after maximizing coverage.
- `calculateWorkpackageAllocationCost` resolves actual monthly allocations (including legacy scalar assignments) and sums allocated FTE × `WORKING_HOURS_PER_MONTH` (160) × each member's location hourly rate across active project months. Explicit monthly and maintenance exclusions are respected; inactive/negated workpackages have no cost. Internal members use hourly pricing; external members add salary charges instead, without location hourly costs. Cards derive `_allocationCost` in App.tsx; management rows calculate separately, and team timelines recalculate from their allocation draft. `WorkpackageCostLabel` uses the configured currency and appears only for a positive priced allocation cost; no-allocation, zero-cost, and fully unpriced summaries stay hidden (`hasAllocatedCost`). Partially priced allocations retain a partial amount; the tooltip explains missing rates. Explicit zero hourly rates are valid. It reports allocated resource cost, not the cost of unmet demand. Assigned workpackage card footers show effort and the yellow cost tag on one row labelled EFFORTS.
- Changing the FTE cost currency converts entered hourly rates at the latest published rate from Frankfurter v2 (`src/utils/currencyRates.ts`), rounding to two decimals. Only currency codes leave the browser. Blank/zero-only rates need no API call; nonzero rates require a valid positive exchange rate, with an in-memory one-hour cache including reciprocal pairs. The UI shows the source rate/date, blocks edits and Save during conversion, and retains the original currency/values on failure. Requests time out after 10 seconds; Reset and closing cancel pending requests and ignore late responses.
- Workpackage cost tags are yellow, sit beside effort values in cards (including compact cards) and timeline/management rows, and abbreviate amounts with k or M; their tooltips retain the full amount. Member allocation cell tooltips in the team timeline (regular and management rows) show monthly cost, hours, and location hourly rate using `calculateMemberMonthlyAllocationCost` / `formatMemberMonthlyAllocationCost`. The same calculation feeds workpackage totals, rounding only the final sum. Missing rates are explicitly unavailable; zero allocation costs zero even if the rate is unset.
- Unassigned workpackage pool cards hide the cost tag in both expanded and compact layouts. Assigned project cards show the tag only for a positive allocated cost; use FunctionCard's existing `isAssigned` predicate for this visibility rule.
- Team timeline workpackage labels, including management support, omit their effort/FTE summary and show only a right-aligned cost tag when active member allocations have a positive priced cost. They do not show N/A. A valid zero rate remains a calculated cost but its tag stays hidden; partially priced allocations retain their explicit partial label. Project rows and workpackage cards retain their effort values.
- Assigned workpackage type tags show just the subcategory when present; expanded pool cards retain the tool → subcategory label. Assigned expanded tags retain the alteration asterisk and show the full hierarchy in the tooltip. Existing compact subcategory abbreviations remain unchanged.
- Compact workpackage cost tags omit the currency, round thousands to whole k and millions to at most one decimal M. Expanded tags retain the currency and up to two decimals; all cost tooltips retain the full amount and currency. Tool type header cost tags retain the standard 10px font and identical expanded formatting (including decimals and currency) in both expanded and compact project layouts. Individual collapsed workpackage cost tags retain their compact size and formatting.
- Management Support Overhead cost tags use the `management` tone with a yellow background, purple text, and purple border in project summaries, including retro mode. Team timelines use the standard yellow cost tag for both management support and regular workpackages.
- Cost tags precede FTE badges in workpackage cards and project tool headers. The overall management support header has no cost tag. Individual management support summary rows place their cost tag in the right-hand group directly before coverage status.
- Project tool headers show summed workpackage allocation costs plus that tool's management support cost beside their FTE/yr badge. `sumWorkpackageAllocationCosts` combines full numeric cost summaries, hours, and unique missing locations so partial/unavailable rates remain visible. Tool totals include only active cards in that project and omit negated cards. Header cost tags retain the same size and value formatting in both layout modes; empty, zero-cost, and fully unpriced totals stay hidden. ProjectBasket calculates overhead costs once per tool and shares them with individual management rows and tool totals.
- Project headers show a darker purple `Cost:` label immediately below Total, summing workpackage costs and management support once in the same tool scope as Total. `ProjectCostSummary` displays whole, unabbreviated EUR amounts with a leading yellow € (including € 0 for an unallocated project) in a compact button with a subtle purple background and a dark purple border. Both the € sign and amount are yellow; the amount retains its larger text size. Clicking the amount opens `ProjectSpendingModal` in the same scope. Non-EUR totals convert using the shared `useEuroCostConversion` hook and existing cached exchange-rate service with cancellation and a 10-second timeout; pending conversion displays … and failed conversion displays N/A. Fully unpriced allocations also display N/A, while partial priced totals retain their number and explain missing rates in the tooltip. External monthly salary metadata remains excluded.
- Project Spending forecasts costs from current FTE allocations and scheduled non-FTE payments; it does not track actual expenses or the cost of unmet demand. FTE costs are read-only; non-FTE payments can be freely overridden in a local draft. It shows monthly and cumulative EUR spending, full project/tool/workpackage totals, average monthly spending, and the peak priced month. Tool sections expand to workpackages and management support; workpackages expand to member costs. Whole amounts are shown in cells, with full two-decimal amounts and allocated hours in tooltips. Missing hourly rates are explicitly flagged with partial amounts or N/A; zero hourly rates remain valid. Empty plans remain accessible from the € 0 button. Hidden and negated workpackages are excluded, and management demand uses the same scoped overheads and custom monthly overrides as the header cost calculation.
- Team-specific spending views include an Other workpackage only when a member whose tool matches the selected team has a positive resolved allocation in an active workpackage month. Zero or inactive assignments do not qualify; missing or zero hourly rates do not affect eligibility. With no qualifying workpackages, the Other section is absent. Project cost buttons, timeline totals, and graph totals share the filtered spending calculation; overall project spending remains unchanged.
- Spending summary cells (tools, monthly, cumulative) use whole thousands with a `k` suffix and no grouping separators at EUR values of one million or more. Summary tags and tooltips retain full amounts. Workpackage/member cells retain full numbers and shrink their font to fit, using ResizeObserver and font-load measurements; they regain their normal font size when space allows. Expand All / Collapse All controls tool rows only, while Spending Tracks and individual Expand Spending buttons control member rows.
- Collapsed assigned project cards measure their header's available width with ResizeObserver. If the category/milestone, effort, cost, and delete controls cannot all fit without truncating the category, hide only the effort badge and retain cost. The invisible effort badge remains measurable to avoid hide/show oscillation; font loading, content changes, themes, and resizing trigger recalculation. Pool and expanded cards do not use this rule. With no visible cost tag, the effort badge is restored and no empty cost wrapper occupies header space.
- The Assign Members dialog edits the timeline's monthly allocation draft. Member inputs set a monthly FTE ceiling; Fill and 100% fill each month's remaining demand within personal capacity. The summary reports the average allocated FTE over the project duration. Saving unchanged preserves existing cell/phase allocations; Clear removes both scalar and monthly assignments. Capacity checks include overlapping projects, management work, and Other workpackages only when Include "Other" WPs is enabled.
- Include "Other" WPs controls team allocation scope, not just row visibility. Turning it off removes this team's scalar and monthly Other assignments in the draft, freeing capacity while preserving other teams' allocations and maintenance preferences. Hidden Other workpackages are excluded from manual allocation, both Magic Wands, priority candidates, coverage summaries, and capacity totals. App.tsx remembers the setting per team in session state when Save & Close is used; Discard keeps the previous setting and assignments. Legacy scalar allocations are materialized before removal so other members' monthly contributions do not change.
- When Other workpackages are included, each row has Exclude to remove it from this team's scope and release this team's allocations. Excluded rows move to the bottom of their project as compact muted placeholders with Include to restore them; no assignment, cell editing, or drag-and-drop is available on these rows. All allocation calculations use the same scope predicate, including priorities, totals, and summaries. App.tsx saves excluded card IDs per team alongside the global Include toggle; Save & Close / Discard applies to both. Re-including does not restore allocations that were released.
- The individual percentage allocation dialog defaults to including maintenance for new allocations and remembers the saved checkbox setting per member and workpackage (including management overhead per project/tool). This preference follows the timeline draft's Save & Close / Discard behavior. Turning it off writes explicit zero allocations for both initial and residual maintenance months when saved. Its percentage presets and maximum consider remaining member capacity and workpackage demand in the included months; saving remains capped separately for each month.
- The percentage editor opens with the largest existing allocation in the included months as its monthly ceiling; do not initialize it from the active-month average displayed in timeline member rows. Its selective-allocation warning detects custom cell values, missing included months, and manually allocated maintenance cells when maintenance is excluded. Intentionally unallocated maintenance alone does not trigger it. Closing the nested editor's backdrop must leave the team timeline draft open.
- The Magic Wand beside each team timeline project badge rebalances the current team's allocations across that project's active workpackages, including Other rows when enabled and management overhead. A residual flow calculation maximizes covered monthly effort while respecting roles, other projects' commitments, and saved per-member maintenance exclusions. It preserves other teams' assignments and only updates the local timeline draft; Save & Close applies it. The result status reports full or partial coverage.
- The team timeline header uses icon-only buttons for Auto-allocate team (Magic Wand), Clear Allocation (reset arrow), and Include "Other" WPs (Other tool cube), with accessible labels and hover explanations. Include "Other" WPs sits immediately before Manual Adjust and retains its highlighted active state.
- The team-level Magic Wand opens `TeamAllocationPriorityModal.tsx` and offers only projects with active workpackages in the team's timeline scope. Priority 1 is highest; equal priorities are optimized jointly as one group on the shared calendar. Higher priority groups reserve each member's monthly capacity before lower groups are processed. Projects outside the optimization retain their allocations and reserve capacity. These changes also remain in the timeline draft.
- Priority optimization uses one residual graph per calendar month with a node for each priority group. Groups are enabled from highest to lowest priority; subsequent groups may reroute members within earlier groups while preserving their total coverage. Do not freeze individual member assignments after optimizing a group: that can unnecessarily leave lower priority tasks uncovered when member eligibility differs. Equal priorities maximize their combined covered FTE, without a fairness or equal-share constraint.
- Both Magic Wands pass the current `fteCosts` to the shared optimizer. After maximum coverage is established for every priority group, a minimum-cost flow pass locks those group coverage totals and minimizes location hourly rate × allocated FTE (the common 160 hours/month factor does not change the optimum). It uses shortest residual paths with potentials and a binary heap, preserving roles, monthly capacity, fixed other-team/project assignments, maintenance exclusions, and team scope. Cost never trades away coverage at any priority. Missing/invalid rates receive a conservative penalty rather than a free price, but remain eligible when required for coverage; explicit zero rates are valid. External monthly salaries remain metadata and are excluded from this objective. If numerical residuals prevent satisfying a locked group, restore the original maximum-coverage flow.
- Preserve a month's existing assignments when they satisfy all role, maintenance, demand and capacity limits and already achieve the optimized priority coverage and cost. This prevents repeated Magic Wand clicks from reshuffling equally optimal plans. Validate the input before preserving it so overbooked or ineligible assignments are still repaired. Cost comparisons use floating-point-relative tolerance; shortest-path distances must not use a fixed absolute tolerance that could hide small rate differences after normalization.
- Clear Allocation beside the team Magic Wand removes the current team's scalar and monthly assignments across its workpackages (including negated rows and Other when enabled) and management overhead. It preserves other teams' assignments and maintenance preferences, and follows Save & Close / Discard draft semantics. After auto-allocation, the footer shows live coverage per active project, including management overhead and Other workpackages when enabled. Workpackage and allocation cells with no required effort use the outside-project dotted appearance; active negated workpackages retain their muted styling.
- In the team timeline, dropping a member onto a workpackage label fills all its months, onto a month cell fills that month, and onto the invisible 24px-wide drop zone before a subactivity or its first phase label fills only that contiguous phase. Hovering during a member drag highlights the phase and shows its month range. These phase targets exist for phases spanning multiple months, including phases lasting the entire project duration such as management overhead. Each month's allocation is capped by the member's remaining capacity and the effort left after other members' coverage. Manual edits and resets use the same limits.
- `Other` workpackages have custom monthly effort, duration, start/boundary information, and an optional maintenance phase.
- Reusability supports an Other selection with `customReusabilityFactor` from 0 to 1 inclusive. Creation and editing validate it, normalize decimal commas to dots, and convert matching configured factors to the predefined reusability label on save. Custom factor forms offer `reusabilityAppliesToMaintenance` for workpackages with maintenance, off by default. This scales initial/residual or Other maintenance while leaving support unchanged. Matching preset labels preserve the flag; editing such a card reopens the custom controls so users can change it. Choosing a preset directly clears the flag. Use `getReusabilityFactor`, `getMaintenanceReusabilityFactor`, and `getReusabilityLabel` consistently in calculations and previews. Pass configured factors and FTE rates through card editors.
- Calculations and defaults should come from `src/utils/helpers.ts` and `src/constants/index.ts` where applicable. Avoid reimplementing existing effort or milestone math in a component.
- A reusability factor of exactly 0 also removes Functions Dev Support and Weekly Meetings effort. Use `getSupportReusabilityFactor` in defaults, totals, coverage, timelines, and allocation reconciliation. Saved support overrides are ignored at factor 0 and remain available if the factor later becomes positive. Positive factors leave support unchanged; maintenance still follows its separate opt-in setting.
- Decimal entry for custom `Other` effort and maintenance uses text inputs with `inputMode="decimal"` so the displayed separator is a dot; comma keystrokes are normalized to a dot.
- Expanded workpackage cards shorten the custom reusability tag from Other Reusability to Other when the full tag and name would not fit on their shared row. A separate invisible full label is measured with ResizeObserver so the long form returns when space allows without oscillation. The factor and × stay blue, the tooltip retains the full label, and predefined/factor-only timeline labels are unchanged.

- `externalSalaries.ts` provides salary shares and unused paid-capacity calculations. `ExternalSalaryUtilization` in Project Spending displays each member’s monthly salary share, paid FTE, allocated FTE, unused FTE, and the already-included unused salary cost. Unused capacity uses max(0, member FTE - all project allocations), shared in the same proportions as salary. Salaries remain read-only in Non-FTE Adjust; only purchase schedules can be edited. `calculatePortfolioSalaryTotals` supplies the common calendar-month allocation denominator to dashboard/project/team draft costs. Missing salary and exchange rates remain unavailable rather than using a location-rate fallback.

## Non-FTE purchases

- Workpackages have optional `kind`; missing or `fte` retains the existing staffing behavior, while `non-fte` is a purchase. Purchase types are License, Workstation, Hardware, and Contracted workpackage.
- `NonFteWorkpackageModal` creates/edits EUR prices, required supplier, tool, reusability, and optional milestone deadline. Existing purchases edit inline in the pool or project card, using the FTE editor styling and Save/Cancel flow; new purchases use the creation modal. Creation and editing require positive configured base and final EUR costs after cent rounding; manual spending overrides may independently reduce the displayed total to zero; a zero reusability factor cannot be saved for a purchase. Inline editing uses the configured supplier list and retains deadline rescheduling before applying edits. Suppliers live in session configuration, with initial Luxoft, T&S, Akoddis, and rProcess entries; Defaults → Suppliers supports add/remove. Suppliers referenced by any non-FTE workpackage, including unassigned or hidden purchases, cannot be removed. Reset to Defaults and configuration saving also preserve referenced supplier records.
- The pool's FTE / Non-FTE switch filters its cards. Extended-mode project headers have icon-only FTE, non-FTE, and combined view filters, defaulting to both. Basic mode hides these filters and always displays both kinds, while retaining the previous Extended-mode selection for switching back. These only filter displayed cards; project and tool totals, allocations, and spending remain unchanged. Non-FTE-only views hide management overhead summaries. Project tools group receipt-style purchases below FTE workpackages within their matching subtool when available. Purchase creation/editing selects a subtool; changing tools resets it to a valid default. Legacy purchases without a valid subtool use the tool’s first subtool. Hidden subtools negate their purchases and exclude them from costs and spending. Pool tags show tool → subtool, and assigned tags show the subtool only. Tool tag text is light red; its dot reflects price after reusability: ≤ €50k green, ≤ €200k yellow, otherwise red.
- Receipt cards use a white background, tool-colored border and type icon, and an amber dashed bottom edge. Expanded cards show the supplier beside the name, then a Payments disclosure and TOTAL COST. Clicking Payments animates the monthly rows open/closed; reduced-motion preferences disable the animation. Monthly rows use a receipt-style Courier font and grayscale cost tags; the total tag stays yellow. Compact cards show just the type icon, name, and cost, without supplier, edit, payment controls, or the TOTAL COST label. FTE and non-FTE cards share their layout widths.
- `AssignNonFteModal` follows the Other scheduling dialog styling with milestone target buttons and a compact horizontal project preview, without a starting-month field. It requires one or more one-based project payment months, limited to the normalized milestone deadline (inclusive) or project end. Clicks toggle individual months; dragging selects or removes a range according to its first month while preserving selections outside that range. Changing to an earlier deadline removes now-invalid draft months; confirming saves both the selected deadline and payment months, while Cancel discards them. `nonFteWorkpackages.ts` splits the effective price in cents deterministically across selected months. One month uses At once; multiple months default to Evenly distributed. Split mode collects positive EUR amounts for every selected month, caps entries at the remaining balance, and requires their cent totals to equal the full final price before confirming. It saves `purchasePaymentMode` and fractional `purchasePaymentShares`; these retain the custom proportions after price/reusability changes. Spending calculations distribute rounded cents by largest remainder so monthly payments exactly match the final price. Existing cards without payment metadata retain even splitting. Removing a month frees its draft payment, and returning to the pool clears payment metadata. Moving to the pool clears the payment schedule. Editing to an earlier deadline requires rescheduling; project edits that invalidate payments are blocked.
- Purchases are excluded from FTE effort, lifecycle support, management triggers, staffing timelines, allocation reconciliation, and Magic Wands. External members remain eligible for ordinary staffing allocations; their full salaries contribute separately to non-FTE spending.
- `WorkpackageAllocationCost.purchaseCostEUR` carries purchases separately from `totalCost`, which remains in the FTE rate currency. `externalSalaryCharges` carries salary currency, paid capacity, local allocated effort and portfolio-wide allocated effort keyed by member/calendar month. Aggregators merge the allocation shares without duplicating salary. Aggregate EUR displays convert the FTE portion and each saved salary currency separately, then add EUR purchases. Never multiply purchase amounts by the FTE currency exchange rate.
- Spending tracks flag `isNonFte` and have no member tracks. Overall graph tool segments include purchases; tool-specific breakdowns show a separate Non-FTE purchases segment. Engineering and FTE readouts exclude purchases and external salaries. The non-FTE spending series includes both.
- Spending timeline Non-FTE Adjust freely edits payment amounts in any project month, including empty cells and months after the original milestone. No original-price or remaining-balance limit applies. Draft amounts use integer EUR cents; saved purchasePaymentOverrides store absolute EUR amounts by one-based month, independently of base price/reusability and the original month/mode/share schedule. Zero removes a payment, including all payments. Monthly, cumulative, tool, graph, receipt, and overview totals use the overrides. Save & Close validates all edited schedules before atomically updating the workpackages; all other close routes discard the draft. Empty cells show an input only while being edited, and toggling adjustment preserves display typography. _isAltered compares the saved schedule with its configured baseline; overview category tags append *, timeline names show a red *, and changed cells show red rings and stars with default amounts in tooltips. Reset restores the configured schedule in the draft and saving clears the overrides. The calendar scheduling dialog retains its milestone/price limits, reallocating the effective overridden total when editing an altered purchase. Returning a purchase to the pool clears its manual overrides. Project milestone edits preserve manual overrides; duration edits cannot cut off a positive payment. Non-FTE spending labels show a light red Non-FTE tag at the top right and omit supplier metadata.

## Implementation guardrails

- Preserve existing behavior, form factors, visual hierarchy, and styling unless the requested feature explicitly calls for a change. The client is strict about visual changes.
- For extraction/refactoring work, keep JSX structure, wrappers, Tailwind classes, and inline styles unchanged unless the user explicitly requests otherwise.
- Keep shared timeline selection mechanics in `useTimelineRangeSelection`, but keep project and team edit semantics in their respective consumers.
- Prefer a focused change in the existing feature component or helper over broad cleanup while implementing a feature. Avoid adding dependencies without a clear need.
- Keep new shared domain types in `src/types/index.ts`, static defaults in `src/constants/index.ts`, pure calculations in the appropriate `src/utils/` module, and reusable UI under `src/components/ui/`. The helper barrel retains existing imports; focused modules should import their dependencies directly to avoid cycles.
- Existing screenshot snapshots are visual regression references. Do not update them merely to make a change pass; update them only when a visual change was requested and reviewed.
- Avoid assuming application data survives a reload. Currently, only the compact-layout flags persist.
